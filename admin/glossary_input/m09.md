# Module 9 glossary input

- **Time-invariant covariate:** a covariate that takes one value per participant for the whole study, such as arm, sex or baseline age.
- **Time-varying covariate:** a covariate that can take a different value at each measurement occasion, such as daily steps or time itself.
- **Person mean:** the mean of a time-varying covariate over a participant's observed occasions; it varies only between participants.
- **Person-mean centring:** subtracting each participant's person mean from their values of a time-varying covariate, so that the deviation varies only within participants.
- **Within-person (within-subject) effect:** the expected change in a participant's outcome when their covariate is one unit above their own average.
- **Between-person (between-subject) effect:** the expected difference in outcome between participants whose person means of the covariate differ by one unit.
- **Contextual effect:** the between-person effect minus the within-person effect; the difference in outcome between two participants with the same current covariate value whose person means differ by one unit.
- **Mediator:** a variable on the causal pathway from treatment to outcome; adjusting for it removes the part of the treatment effect that acts through it.
- **Total effect:** the effect of treatment on the outcome through all pathways; in a randomised trial, the effect of randomisation to an arm.
- **Direct effect:** the part of a treatment effect that does not act through a specified mediator; estimating it requires assumptions beyond randomisation.
- **Reverse causation:** an association that arises because the outcome influences the covariate rather than the other way round.
- **Time-varying confounding:** confounding by a variable that changes over time and affects both a time-varying exposure and the outcome.
- **Unequal measurement intervals:** gaps between measurement occasions that differ in length, by design (months 0, 1, 3, 6, ...) or because actual visit times vary.
- **Basis function:** a function of time (such as $t$, $t^2$ or a spline column) used as a covariate so that a linear model can describe a curved trajectory.
- **Knot:** a time point at which a piecewise or spline function is allowed to change its slope or curvature.
- **Piecewise linear model (linear spline):** a trajectory made of straight-line segments joined at knots, for example $t$ and $(t - 3)_+$.
- **Natural cubic spline:** a smooth curve made of cubic pieces joined at knots and constrained to be linear beyond the outer knots; `splines::ns()` in R.
- **Generalized additive mixed model (GAMM):** a mixed model in which some covariate effects are smooth functions estimated with a roughness penalty, for example `mgcv::gamm()`.
- **Effective degrees of freedom (edf):** a measure of the complexity of a penalised smooth; an edf of 1 corresponds to a straight line.
- **Basis dimension (k):** the maximum number of basis functions used to represent a smooth in `mgcv`; it sets an upper limit on the edf.
