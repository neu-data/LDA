# Module 11 glossary input

- **Conditional residual:** the observed outcome minus the participant's own predicted value (fixed effects plus predicted random effects); it estimates the residual error and is shrunk towards zero.
- **Marginal residual:** the observed outcome minus the population-average prediction from the fixed effects alone; it contains both the participant's random effects and the residual error, so marginal residuals of one participant are correlated.
- **Standardised conditional residual:** a conditional residual divided by the estimated residual SD.
- **Cholesky-scaled marginal residuals:** marginal residuals multiplied by the inverse Cholesky factor of a participant's fitted marginal covariance matrix; approximately independent standard normal values if the mean and covariance models are correct.
- **Scale-location plot:** a plot of the square root of the absolute standardised residuals against fitted values, used to check whether the residual variance changes with the level of the outcome.
- **Influential participant:** a participant whose removal changes a reported estimate materially, judged relative to the estimate's standard error.
- **Leave-one-participant-out (LOO) analysis:** refitting a model once for each participant with that participant's data omitted, to measure influence.
- **DFBETA:** the change in a specific estimate when one participant (or observation) is omitted.
- **Cook's distance (for fixed effects):** the shift in all fixed effects when a participant is omitted, scaled by their estimated covariance matrix and divided by the number of fixed effects.
- **Shrinkage:** the pulling of predicted random effects (BLUPs) towards zero by the factor tau^2 / (tau^2 + sigma^2 / n_i); stronger for participants with few or noisy observations.
- **Shrinkage factor:** the weight lambda_i given to a participant's own data in their BLUP; it lies between 0 and 1.
- **Scaled (quantile) residuals (DHARMa):** the position of each observed value within the distribution of values simulated from the fitted model; uniform on (0, 1) if the model is correct, for any outcome type.
- **Conditional versus unconditional simulation (DHARMa):** simulating new responses with the predicted random effects held fixed, or with new random effects drawn each time; participant-level checks need unconditional simulation.
- **Overdispersion:** more variation in a count or binomial outcome than the assumed distribution allows; it makes model-based standard errors too small.
- **Zero inflation:** more zero counts than the assumed distribution predicts; often a symptom of overdispersion rather than a separate process.
- **Convergence warning:** a message that the optimiser's stopping point may not be the maximum of the likelihood, usually because the gradient there exceeds a tolerance.
- **Gradient (in model fitting):** the slope of the log-likelihood with respect to the parameters; zero at a maximum.
- **allFit():** an lme4 function that refits a model with every available optimiser so that their log-likelihoods and estimates can be compared.
- **Singular fit:** a fit in which a variance is estimated as zero or a correlation as plus or minus one; the random-effects structure is too complex for the data.
- **Separation:** a pattern in binary data in which a predictor or combination of predictors perfectly predicts the outcome, so the maximum-likelihood estimate is infinite.
- **Firth's penalised likelihood:** maximisation of the log-likelihood plus half the log-determinant of the Fisher information; it gives finite estimates under separation and reduces small-sample bias.
- **Adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature (nAGQ):** a more accurate numerical approximation than the Laplace approximation to the likelihood of a GLMM with a single random effect.
- **Analysis log:** a dated record of each analytical decision, what prompted it, its status (pre-specified, data-driven but blind, post hoc) and its effect on the main estimate.
