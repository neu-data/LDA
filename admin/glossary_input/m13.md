# Module 13 glossary input (advanced extensions)

- **Ordinal outcome:** a categorical outcome whose categories have a natural order (for example none, mild, moderate, severe) but whose spacing is not defined.
- **Cumulative logit:** the log-odds of being in category k or below, $\operatorname{logit} P(Y \le k)$; the basis of proportional odds models.
- **Cut-point (threshold):** in a cumulative-logit model, one of the K − 1 ordered values $\theta_k$ that separate adjacent categories of the outcome on the latent scale; the cut-points replace a single intercept.
- **Cumulative-logit mixed model (cumulative link mixed model):** a model for a repeated ordinal outcome that combines cumulative logits with random effects, fitted in R with `ordinal::clmm()`.
- **Proportional odds assumption:** the assumption that each covariate has the same odds ratio at every cut-point, so one coefficient describes "higher versus lower" categories whatever the cut-off.
- **Cumulative odds ratio:** the odds ratio for being in a higher rather than a lower category, which under proportional odds is the same for every cut-point.
- **Nominal effect (partial proportional odds):** a covariate allowed to have a different coefficient at each cut-point; used to relax or test the proportional odds assumption.
- **Nominal (multinomial) outcome:** a categorical outcome whose categories have no natural order.
- **Baseline-category logit model (multinomial logit):** a model for a nominal outcome in which the log-odds of each category versus a reference category has its own set of coefficients; with random effects it becomes a multinomial GLMM.
- **Three-level model:** a mixed model with random effects at three nested levels, for example visits within participants within clinics.
- **Variance partition:** the division of the total outcome variance into the shares attributable to each level of a multilevel model.
- **Cluster-randomised trial:** a trial in which whole groups (clinics, schools) rather than individuals are allocated to arms, so that treatment is constant within each group.
- **Nonlinear mixed-effects model:** a mixed model in which the mean function is nonlinear in at least one parameter (for example a time constant inside an exponential), so it cannot be written as $X\boldsymbol\beta + Z\mathbf{b}$; fitted in R with `nlme::nlme()`.
- **Asymptotic exponential model:** a curve that starts at a baseline level and approaches a plateau, $\phi_0 - \phi_1(1 - e^{-t/\kappa})$, with plateau change $\phi_1$ and time constant $\kappa$.
- **Time constant:** in an exponential approach to a plateau, the time by which about 63% of the eventual change has occurred; about 95% has occurred by three time constants.
- **Starting values:** initial guesses for the parameters of an iterative fitting algorithm; nonlinear models need them and may converge to different solutions from poor starts.
- **Joint model (longitudinal and time-to-event):** a model that links a mixed model for a repeated biomarker and a survival model for an event through shared random effects, so that the hazard depends on the biomarker's true trajectory.
- **Association parameter:** in a joint model, the coefficient linking a feature of the biomarker trajectory (for example its current value) to the log hazard of the event.
- **Functional form (joint model):** the feature of the biomarker trajectory that enters the hazard, such as the current value, the current slope or the cumulative area.
- **Time-dependent Cox model:** a proportional hazards model whose covariates change over follow-up; with a biomarker measured at visits it usually carries the last observed value forward.
- **Regression dilution:** attenuation of an estimated association towards zero because the covariate is measured with error or is out of date.
- **Dynamic prediction:** an individual's predicted future biomarker values or event risk that is updated each time a new measurement becomes available.
- **Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC):** a simulation method that draws samples from a posterior distribution; results depend on the chains having converged.
- **Burn-in:** the initial MCMC iterations that are discarded because the chains may not yet have reached the posterior distribution.
- **Rhat (Gelman-Rubin statistic):** a convergence diagnostic that compares variation within and between MCMC chains; values close to 1 (below about 1.05) indicate agreement.
- **Trace plot:** a plot of sampled parameter values against iteration for each MCMC chain, used to check mixing and convergence.
- **Time-varying confounding:** confounding by a variable that changes over time, influences later treatment and the outcome, and may itself be affected by earlier treatment.
- **Directed acyclic graph (DAG):** a diagram of assumed causal relationships between variables, with arrows from causes to effects and no cycles.
- **Collider:** a variable that is a common effect of two other variables on a path; conditioning on it creates an association between its causes.
- **Potential outcome:** the outcome a participant would have had under a specified treatment or treatment history, whether or not that treatment was received.
- **Marginal structural model (MSM):** a model for the mean potential outcome as a function of treatment history, estimated in a population reweighted to remove measured confounding.
- **Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPW):** weighting each participant by the inverse of the probability of the treatment (history) they received given their covariates, to create a pseudo-population without measured confounding.
- **Stabilised weight:** an inverse probability weight whose numerator is the probability of the received treatment given treatment history only; it keeps weights near 1 and reduces their variability.
- **Sequential exchangeability:** the assumption that, at each time, treatment is independent of the potential outcomes given the measured past (no unmeasured confounding of treatment decisions).
- **Positivity:** the assumption that every participant has a non-zero probability of receiving each treatment level at each time, given their history.
- **G-methods:** methods for time-varying treatments and confounders: inverse probability weighting of marginal structural models, the parametric g-formula and g-estimation of structural nested models.
