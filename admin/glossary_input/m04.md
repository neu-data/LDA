# Module 4 glossary input

- **Linear mixed-effects model (LMM):** a regression model for a continuous outcome that combines fixed effects for the mean with random effects for subject-specific departures from it; also called a multilevel or hierarchical linear model.
- **Fixed effects:** the regression coefficients $\boldsymbol\beta$ describing the mean outcome and how it depends on covariates; in a linear mixed model they have both a population-average and a subject-specific interpretation.
- **Random effects:** subject-specific deviations from the mean trajectory (for example $b_{0i}$, $b_{1i}$), treated as draws from a distribution whose variance is estimated.
- **Random intercept:** a random effect that shifts a subject's whole trajectory up or down relative to the mean trajectory.
- **Random slope:** a random effect that lets a subject's rate of change over time differ from the mean rate of change.
- **Residual error:** the deviation of a single measurement from the subject's own trajectory; its variance is $\sigma^2$.
- **Variance components:** the variances and covariances of the random effects ($\tau_0^2$, $\tau_1^2$, $\tau_{01}$) and the residual variance $\sigma^2$.
- **$G$ matrix:** the covariance matrix of the random effects $\mathbf{b}_i$.
- **Marginal covariance ($V_i$):** the covariance of a subject's repeated measurements implied by the model, $V_i = Z_iGZ_i^\top + R_i$; with independent residuals $R_i = \sigma^2 I$.
- **Intraclass correlation (ICC):** in a random-intercept model, $\tau_0^2/(\tau_0^2 + \sigma^2)$: the share of variance between subjects and the implied correlation between any two measurements of a subject. A random-slope model has no single ICC.
- **Subject-specific (conditional) interpretation:** the meaning of the model for a subject with given random effects.
- **Population-average (marginal) interpretation:** the meaning of the model averaged over subjects; coincides with the subject-specific one for the fixed effects of a linear mixed model.
- **Centring (time):** subtracting a constant from time so that the intercept and main effects refer to that time; it changes the meaning of coefficients, not the model.
- **Categorical time:** time entered as a factor (one mean per visit and group), making no assumption about the shape of the trajectory.
- **Saturated mean model:** a mean model with one parameter per group and visit, able to reproduce any pattern of means.
- **Estimated marginal means (EMMs):** model-based means for combinations of factors, obtained with `emmeans`, from which contrasts such as the difference in mean change are computed.
- **Difference in mean change:** the difference between groups in the mean change from baseline to a given visit; the primary estimand in WALK.
- **Maximum likelihood (ML):** estimation that maximises the full likelihood; variance components are biased downward in small samples; needed to compare models with different fixed effects.
- **Restricted maximum likelihood (REML):** estimation of variance components from the likelihood of residual contrasts, which accounts for the degrees of freedom used by the fixed effects; the default for reporting.
- **Boundary problem:** when a null hypothesis sets a variance to zero, the edge of its allowed range, so likelihood-ratio statistics follow a mixture of chi-square distributions rather than the usual one.
- **Best linear unbiased predictor (BLUP):** the predicted value of a subject's random effects given the data and the fitted model.
- **Shrinkage (partial pooling):** the pulling of subject-specific predictions toward the mean, stronger when a subject has few or noisy observations.
- **Satterthwaite approximation:** a method for approximate degrees of freedom of t- and F-tests in mixed models, used by `lmerTest`.
- **Singular fit:** a fit in which a variance component is estimated as zero or a correlation as ±1, often because the data cannot support the random-effects structure (Module 11).
