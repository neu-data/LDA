# Module 1 glossary input

- **Cross-sectional data:** each unit is measured once.
- **Repeated measures:** more than one measurement of the outcome on the same unit, whether or not time order is of interest.
- **Longitudinal data:** repeated measurements on the same subjects over time, where change over time is of interest.
- **Clustered data:** units grouped within higher-level units (for example patients within clinics) whose members resemble each other; a participant with repeated measurements is a cluster.
- **Subject (participant):** the unit measured repeatedly and assumed independent of other subjects, indexed $i$.
- **Observation:** one measurement of the outcome; one row of a long-format dataset, written $y_{ij}$.
- **Visit:** a scheduled contact defined by the study protocol (for example month 6).
- **Measurement occasion:** the time at which a measurement was actually taken, $t_{ij}$.
- **Scheduled time / actual time:** the protocol time of a visit (`month_sched`) versus when it actually took place (`month`).
- **Long format / wide format:** one row per observation versus one row per subject with a column for each occasion.
- **Balanced design:** every subject is scheduled to be measured at the same set of times.
- **Unbalanced design:** subjects have different numbers or timings of measurements, through design, missed visits or dropout.
- **Intermittent missingness:** a missed visit after which the subject returns.
- **Dropout:** permanent loss of a subject from follow-up (monotone missingness).
- **Unequal follow-up:** subjects are observed over different lengths of time.
- **Informative visit timing:** visit times that depend on the outcome history, as in KIDNEY.
- **Within-subject (within-person) variation:** variation of a subject's measurements around their own level, estimated by the residual variance $\sigma^2$.
- **Between-subject (between-person) variation:** variation of subjects' own levels around the mean, estimated by the random-intercept variance $\tau_0^2$.
- **Variance components:** the separate variances (for example $\tau_0^2$ and $\sigma^2$) into which a mixed model divides the outcome's variance.
- **Random intercept:** a subject-specific deviation $b_{0i}$ of the subject's level from the mean, shared by all of that subject's observations.
- **Fixed effects:** the parameters $\boldsymbol\beta$ of the mean structure, common to all subjects.
- **Intraclass correlation (ICC):** $\tau_0^2/(\tau_0^2+\sigma^2)$, the share of variance between subjects and, under a random-intercept model, the correlation of two measurements on the same subject.
- **Compound symmetry:** a correlation pattern in which every pair of measurements on a subject has the same correlation.
- **Estimand:** the precise quantity a study aims to estimate, stating the population, the contrast, the times and the scale.
- **Difference in mean change:** the difference between groups in the mean change from baseline to a given time; the WALK primary estimand.
- **Population-average (marginal) / subject-specific (conditional):** effects on the mean outcome in a population versus effects on an individual's expected outcome given their random effects.
- **Design effect:** the factor, about $1+(m-1)\rho_I$, by which correlation inflates the variance of a between-subject comparison relative to independent observations.
- **Monte Carlo error:** the sampling error of a summary computed from a finite number of simulation replicates.
- **Coverage:** the proportion of simulated confidence intervals that contain the true value.
