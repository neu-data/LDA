# Module 3 glossary input

- **Interaction (arm-by-time):** a model term that allows the effect of time to differ between groups; in a trial with repeated measurements it carries the treatment effect on change.
- **Main effect (in a model with an interaction):** the effect of one variable when the other variables in the interaction equal zero or their reference level; for example, the arm difference at month 0.
- **Paired t-test:** a test of whether the mean within-person difference between two occasions is zero; it answers a within-group question.
- **Within-group change:** the mean change over time in one group; it mixes any treatment effect with regression to the mean and other time effects.
- **Repeated-measures ANOVA (RM-ANOVA):** analysis of variance for outcomes measured at the same fixed occasions on every subject, with between-subject and within-subject error strata; it requires complete data.
- **Listwise deletion:** removing every subject with any missing value from the analysis; also called complete-case analysis.
- **Compound symmetry:** a covariance structure with equal variances at all occasions and equal correlations between all pairs of occasions.
- **Sphericity:** the condition that the variance of the difference between any two occasions is the same for all pairs; required for the within-subject F-tests of RM-ANOVA.
- **Mauchly's test:** a test of sphericity; it has low power in small samples and rejects trivially in large ones.
- **Greenhouse–Geisser correction:** multiplies the degrees of freedom of within-subject F-tests by an estimate of epsilon (between 1/(k - 1) and 1) to correct for non-sphericity; somewhat conservative.
- **Huynh–Feldt correction:** a less conservative estimate of the same epsilon, never smaller than the Greenhouse–Geisser value and truncated at 1.
- **Epsilon (sphericity):** a measure of departure from sphericity; 1 means sphericity holds.
- **Follow-up-only analysis:** comparison of groups on the outcome at a single follow-up time, ignoring baseline.
- **Change-score analysis:** comparison of groups on the change from baseline to follow-up.
- **Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA, baseline-adjusted):** regression of the follow-up outcome on group and the baseline value; in a randomised trial it is the most precise of the three single-follow-up analyses.
- **Regression to the mean:** the tendency of subjects selected for extreme values to have values nearer the mean when measured again, because part of the extreme value was chance.
- **Lord's paradox:** in non-randomised comparisons, change-score analysis and ANCOVA can lead to different conclusions from the same data because they answer different questions.
- **Multiplicity:** the increase in the chance of at least one false-positive result when many tests are performed.
- **Family-wise error rate:** the probability of at least one false-positive result among a set of tests.
