# Module 10 glossary input

- **Intermittent missingness:** a missed measurement occasion after which the participant returns to the study.
- **Dropout (monotone missingness):** a participant leaves the study and all later measurements are missing.
- **Missingness pattern:** the set of observed and missing occasions for a participant, for example `OOO...` for dropout after the third visit.
- **Missing completely at random (MCAR):** the probability of missingness does not depend on any outcome values, observed or unobserved (it may depend on covariates in the model).
- **Missing at random (MAR):** given the observed data, the probability of missingness does not depend on the unobserved values.
- **Missing not at random (MNAR):** the probability of missingness depends on unobserved values even after conditioning on the observed data.
- **Ignorability:** the condition (MAR plus separability of parameters) under which likelihood-based inference can ignore the missingness mechanism.
- **Separability (distinctness) of parameters:** the parameters of the outcome model and of the missingness model have no common elements or joint constraints.
- **Complete-case analysis:** an analysis restricted to participants with complete data on the variables used; valid in general only under MCAR.
- **Last observation carried forward (LOCF):** single imputation that replaces missing values after dropout with the last observed value; generally biased and overconfident.
- **Multiple imputation (MI):** replacing each missing value with several draws from its predictive distribution, analysing each completed data set and combining the results with Rubin's rules.
- **Rubin's rules:** the formulas that combine multiple-imputation results: the average estimate, and a total variance equal to the within-imputation variance plus (1 + 1/m) times the between-imputation variance.
- **Fraction of missing information:** the proportion of the total variance of an MI estimate that is due to missing data.
- **Congenial imputation model:** an imputation model that contains at least the variables and interactions of the analysis model.
- **Multilevel (two-level) imputation:** imputation of long-format data with a mixed model that represents the clustering of occasions within participants, for example `2l.norm` in `mice`.
- **Auxiliary variable:** a variable used in the imputation model to predict missing values or missingness, but not part of the analysis model.
- **Pattern-mixture model:** a model that specifies the outcome distribution separately for each missingness pattern; delta adjustment is a simple example.
- **Delta adjustment:** a sensitivity analysis that shifts imputed values (for example post-dropout values) by a fixed amount δ from their MAR prediction.
- **Tipping point:** the smallest departure from MAR (value of δ) at which the study conclusion changes.
- **Informative visit timing (outcome-dependent visit process):** a situation in which the timing or frequency of measurements depends on the outcome, for example more frequent visits after a poor result.
